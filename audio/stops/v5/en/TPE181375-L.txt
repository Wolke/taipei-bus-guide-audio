Taipei Children’s Amusement Park (臺北市立兒童新樂園) is our next stop. This corner of Shilin brings together two very different kinds of discovery: the excitement of amusement rides and the pleasure of figuring out how the world works.

The park is about one hundred and nineteen meters from the stop. It has thirteen major rides, along with free play areas and activities for families. Different age groups have their own zones, so the experience is not just about the biggest rides. There are themed performances too, plus a seasonal water play area.

One feature to look for is the Ferris wheel. From high up, it offers views across the park and the surrounding Shilin area. Around dusk, the lights add another layer to the scene.

There is a practical side to all that fun. EasyCard and iPASS can be used for admission, rides, and purchases at designated shops, with top-up facilities available. Food outlets, nursing rooms, accessible routes, and resting seats help make a family visit more comfortable.

Nearby, about one hundred and fifty-seven meters from the stop, is the National Taiwan Science Education Center (國立臺灣科學教育館). Here, the guiding idea is learning by doing.

Permanent exhibitions occupy the third through sixth floors. They explore life science, matter and energy, Earth systems, mathematics, and technology. Models, multimedia, and hands-on equipment turn abstract ideas into things visitors can investigate for themselves.

For a particularly memorable example, there is the sky bicycle, which uses an elevated steel cable to demonstrate balance. Earthquake simulation platforms introduce different levels of shaking and connect that experience with disaster awareness.

Younger children have a dedicated discovery space, while workshops extend the learning into model making, material experiments, and science crafts. Changing special exhibitions bring in further themes from nature, technology, and engineering. Together, these two neighbors offer play, observation, and plenty of opportunities to ask, “How does that work?”
