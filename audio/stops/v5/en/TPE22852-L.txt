Neihu Senior High School (內湖高中) is our next stop. Nearby is Guo Ziyi Memorial Hall, also known as Neihu Red House (郭子儀紀念堂_內湖紅樓), just one hundred and eighty-seven metres from the stop. This hillside home offers a glimpse of how a wealthy household built in Neihu more than a century ago.

The house belonged to the Guo family, whose member served as Neihu’s first village head during Japanese rule. It was built around nineteen nineteen, in the Taisho era. Taiwan was enjoying social stability and a prosperous economy, and the styles used for official buildings were finding their way into private homes.

Here, that influence became a grand residence made of brick and timber. It sits partway up a small hill west of the old Neihu town area, facing south, with woodland surrounding it on three sides. Its floor plan is roughly shaped like the letter T.

There is plenty to notice in the exterior. Red brick gives the house its familiar character, but look more closely at the decorative finishes: washed aggregate and colourful ceramic tiles imported from Japan. These materials were part of the architectural fashion of the time.

The front is especially expressive. The windows come in varied forms, and a curved balcony projects outward. Above them, the ornamental gable has flowing, curling shapes inspired by Baroque design.

Inside, numerous timber beams support the wooden floors. There are also beams intended for hanging lanterns and a traditional incense burner used in worship of the Heavenly God.

One final detail tells of a change that never happened. In more recent years, the gable received an inscription naming Bifeng Temple (碧奉宮). There had been an intention to turn the house into a temple, but that plan was not carried out. A small inscription, then, preserves a very different possible future for this old family home.
