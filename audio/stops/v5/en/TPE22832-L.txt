Taiwan Television (臺視) is our next stop. Nearby, two very different venues tell a story of Taipei’s cultural life, from intimate exhibitions to performances and sport on a much larger scale.

About a hundred and twenty-six metres from the stop is Taipei City Stage (城市舞臺). Its story begins in nineteen sixty-four, with the founding of the Taipei Municipal Social Education Center. Bringing art and culture to the public was its central purpose. The first-floor lobby and three exhibition rooms provide spaces for displays, while the theatre and arts plaza host music, dance and drama.

A major renovation, completed in two thousand and three, gave the performance venue a fresh start under the City Stage name. It became a more modern, professional theatre designed for different kinds of performances. So there are two sides to this cultural space: the quieter experience of looking at exhibitions, and the shared excitement of a live show.

Then there is Taipei Arena (臺北小巨蛋), about three hundred and eighty-two metres from our stop, at Dunhua North Road and Section Four of Nanjing East Road. Before the arena, this site was Taipei’s municipal baseball stadium. The old stadium was demolished at the end of two thousand, making way for a multipurpose venue with fifteen thousand seats.

Its versatility is worth imagining. Retractable seating changes the layout, and the central floor can become an ice surface for competitions. That flexibility helps the arena accommodate major sporting events as well as arts performances.

The separate skating rink measures sixty-one metres by thirty metres. It welcomes recreational skaters, but it is also a training space for skilled athletes and national-team competitors. There is room for four hundred skaters on the ice and eight hundred spectators in the stands. From exhibition rooms to an ice rink, this neighbourhood brings together quite a range of ways to enjoy the city.
