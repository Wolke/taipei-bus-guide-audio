Neishuangxi Nature Center (內雙溪自然中心) is our upcoming stop. Here’s a greener side of Taipei, with a forest landscape covering one hundred and four hectares. In Shilin District (士林區), this is the city’s largest forest nature park. Its rich ecology and varied terrain have even earned it a nickname among hikers: Taipei’s Little Xitou (臺北小溪頭).

There’s a story behind all that natural variety. This area lies where species from the older Xueshan Range (雪山山脈) and the younger Datun Range (大屯山山脈) meet. It is also part of a well-watered catchment. Together, those conditions make this an especially valuable place for wildlife and ecological learning. Think of it as a meeting ground between two mountain worlds.

At the entrance, Bixi Bridge (碧溪橋) offers a few details worth noticing. A stone sculpture of a Taiwan tree frog stands at the bridgehead, drawing attention to the area’s abundant ecology. Beside the bridge is a semicircular viewing platform. From there, the scene opens onto flowing stream water and the unusual rock formations of Shitoushan (獅頭山).

Farther along the walking trails, another viewing structure makes quite an impression. This timber lookout rises six metres and is built entirely from ebony wood. Its four supporting columns keep the distinctive shapes of the original logs. The platform provides broad views toward Dalunwei Mountain (大崙尾山) and Yuanshanzi Mountain (圓山仔山).

Careful city improvements have linked trails, viewing platforms, and access roads into a wider green recreation corridor, connecting mountain areas and nearby rural attractions. So the nature center is part of a larger landscape, rather than an isolated park.

There’s a practical learning element here, too. A solar power demonstration uses simple interactive experiences to introduce energy saving and carbon reduction. Between the water, the woodland, the lookout structures, and the educational features, this is an outdoor classroom with plenty to notice.
