Shilin Police Precinct (士林分局) is our upcoming stop. Around here, temple history, night market food, and outdoor art offer three different ways to discover the neighborhood.

About a hundred and fifty meters from the stop is Shilin Cixian Temple (士林慈諴宮), also known as Shilin’s Mazu temple. It stands within the night market and honors Mazu, the Heavenly Holy Mother. Its story began in seventeen ninety-six, when landowner He Jintang donated land for an earlier temple. In eighteen fifty-nine, fighting between Zhangzhou and Quanzhou groups brought fire to the old streets and destroyed that temple.

Afterward, local leader Pan Yongqing championed rebuilding the neighborhood on a new site. The temple received a central place in that plan. So its location tells a story of a community starting again.

There is plenty to notice in the building itself. Two halls, connecting corridors, and side wings form five courtyards. Look for the entrance hall’s double-eaved roof with its upturned ridges, and the finely painted palace attendants and door guardians by craftsman Chen Yufeng. Beside the main hall, a stone wall depicts Shilin’s old streets.

About three hundred and forty meters from our stop is Shilin Night Market (士林觀光夜市), Taipei’s largest night market. Food and shopping are its big attractions. The snacks range from oversized fried chicken cutlets and grilled skewers to Shilin sausages and pan-fried buns filled with hot juices. There are also spring rolls packed with braised pork and crisp egg, plus warming herbal pork rib soup.

For a greener scene, Fulin Park (福林公園) is about five hundred and eighty meters away. Completed in nineteen ninety-one, this triangular park features six nature-inspired sculpture groups by artist Li Zh Wen: sun, clouds, mountains, waterfall, water, and well. Water is cast in bronze; the others use natural stone. Their abstract, character-inspired forms reward both a distant look and a closer view.
