Our next stop is Shuangxi Park (雙溪公園), about two hundred meters from the park itself. Here’s a little pocket of calm amid the city’s bustle, with a landscape inspired by traditional Chinese gardens.

The park takes its name from two streams that flow southward and meet in Shilin (士林). Completed in nineteen seventy-four, it has around two hectares open to the public. Its grounds are flat and triangular, but the garden design brings together water, rocks, and miniature mountain scenery.

Covered walkways connect a pavilion in the middle of the pond with waterside structures, a stone grotto, and a winding bridge with nine turns. Artificial hills and waterfalls add to the landscape, creating a garden that combines modern touches with an old-fashioned charm.

It’s a compact place with plenty of different features to notice, from the turns of the bridge to the pavilion surrounded by water. A quiet contrast to the busy streets around it.
