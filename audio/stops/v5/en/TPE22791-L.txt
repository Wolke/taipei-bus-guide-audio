Our next stop is Xindong Street Intersection (新東街口). There’s a little pocket of watery nature nearby that offers quite a contrast to Taipei’s busy streets.

About two hundred and thirty-seven meters from the stop is Fumin Ecological Park (富民生態公園). It is one of the few parks in Taipei designed mainly around aquatic ecology. Creating it took about five years, and the work was completed at the end of two thousand and two.

The park sits beside Lane one hundred and seventy-seven of Minsheng East Road, Section Five, between Sanmin Road and Xindong Street. Its grounds cover about eight thousand four hundred square meters. But the feature to picture is a waterway nearly one hundred meters long. It brings together the character of northern Taiwan’s streams in a small urban space.

What makes that waterway interesting is how the habitats change along its course. The stream section leads downstream into a pond habitat. Here, aquatic plants include floating water ferns, water lilies, cattails, and Taiwan yellow water lilies. At the far end, a floodplain habitat adds another variety of water-loving plants. So this is not simply a pond surrounded by greenery. The sequence of habitats is the heart of the park’s design.

And there is more to notice than plants. Birds, amphibians, and insects are part of the life here. The sounds include calling frogs, birdsong, and running water. Fragrance is part of the experience too, with ginger lilies and osmanthus adding their floral scents.

That combination is the park’s particular charm: water to follow with your eyes, wildlife to notice, and natural sounds and scents around you. In a city where daily life so often comes with traffic, Fumin offers residents a chance to slow down and experience nature close to home.
