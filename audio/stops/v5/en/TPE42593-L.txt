Our next stop is Neishuangxi Nature Center (內雙溪自然中心). Here’s a forest world just one hundred and forty-three meters from the stop, with much more to discover than its quiet name might suggest.

Set in Shilin District (士林區), this is Taipei’s largest forest nature park, covering one hundred and four hectares. Its rich wildlife and varied terrain have earned it an affectionate nickname among hikers: Taipei’s Little Xitou (溪頭), after the well-known forest destination.

The city’s restoration work has made this landscape easier to explore through hiking trails, viewing platforms, and access roads. Together, these connect the mountain areas of Shilin and Neihu (內湖), linking nearby camping and farming attractions into a larger green recreation corridor.

One lovely detail appears right at the entrance, at Bixi Bridge (碧溪橋). A stone sculpture of a Taiwan tree frog stands at the bridgehead, a small introduction to the area’s ecological richness. Beside the bridge, a semicircular viewing platform looks down over the flowing stream and the unusual rock formations of Shitou Mountain (獅頭山).

Farther along the trails, the giant timber lookout gives you something quite different to notice. It rises six meters high and is built entirely from ebony wood. Its four supporting pillars retain the distinctive forms of the original logs. From the platform, views open toward Dalunwei Mountain (大崙尾山) and Yuanshanzi Mountain (圓山仔山).

There is a deeper natural story here, too. This water-rich catchment lies where species associated with the older Xueshan Range (雪山山脈) and the younger Datun Range (大屯山山脈) meet. That meeting helps explain why this forest is such a valuable home for living things.

The center also brings environmental learning into everyday life. A solar photovoltaic demonstration system uses simple interactive experiences to introduce energy saving and carbon reduction. Forest scenery, wildlife, water, and practical learning all come together here in one outdoor classroom.
