Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall (中正紀念堂) is our next stop. There’s quite a mix nearby, from grand architecture to performing arts and stories of the sea.

The memorial hall rises seventy meters above its park, with white walls and a blue roof. Built to commemorate Chiang Kai-shek, it was designed by Yang Cho-cheng, who also designed the Grand Hotel. Beneath the hall, there are displays and exhibitions to explore.

Nearby, the National Theater and Concert Hall (國家兩廳院) opened in nineteen eighty-seven as Taiwan’s first national cultural complex. Look for the palace-style buildings, with yellow roof tiles, sweeping eaves, and red columns. Their four performance halls host music, theater, and dance.

And the Evergreen Maritime Museum (長榮海事博物館) brings the ocean into view. Its ship models range from dugout canoes and sailing ships to powered vessels, alongside Taiwan’s bamboo rafts and ceremonial King Boats. Marine paintings and interactive navigation displays add more ways to discover life at sea.
