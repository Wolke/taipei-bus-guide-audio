Shuangxi Park (雙溪公園) is our next stop. About two hundred meters from the stop, this garden offers a quiet pocket of scenery amid the bustle of the city.

Its name comes from two streams that flow from north to south and meet in Shilin (士林). The park opened in nineteen seventy-four, with around two hectares open to the public. The grounds are flat and triangular, but the layout brings together the varied scenery of a traditional Chinese garden.

Covered walkways link a pavilion in the middle of the pond with waterside terraces, a stone grotto, and a bridge with nine turns. Artificial hills and waterfalls add to this miniature landscape of mountains and water.

There is plenty of variety within this compact space. The connected garden features combine a modern sense of beauty with an old-fashioned charm. It is a peaceful contrast to the busy city around it, with water, stone, and garden architecture brought together.
