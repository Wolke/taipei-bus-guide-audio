Our next stop is MRT NTU Hospital Station (捷運台大醫院站). Around here, hospital corridors, park paths, and grand reception rooms tell very different stories about Taipei. Let’s take a closer look.

Nearby is National Taiwan University Hospital’s West Site (國立臺灣大學醫學院附設醫院西址). Founded during Japanese rule, the hospital became central to Taiwan’s modern medical development. Architect Kondo Juro designed this two-storey building with red brick, reinforced concrete, and Renaissance styling. Notice its balanced layout: a central corridor connects the clinics and wards on either side. The spacious, richly decorated hall reflects the ambition behind what was once Southeast Asia’s largest and most modern hospital. This is still a working medical site, with outpatient services and some wards.

Then there is Two Two Eight Peace Memorial Park (二二八和平公園). Its old trees, ponds, and arched bridges offer a gentler rhythm. Local residents practise tai chi or play chess beneath pavilions, while pigeons and squirrels share the grounds. Much of the classical landscaping survives from its Japanese-era life as Taipei New Park. But there is a solemn story here too. The park commemorates the February twenty-eighth Incident. Its central monument expresses hopes for peace and reconciliation, while a former radio station houses the memorial museum.

Inside the park stands the National Taiwan Museum (國立臺灣博物館), Taiwan’s oldest museum. Look for the grand columns and Roman-style dome. In the lofty entrance hall, daylight filters through stained glass overhead. Its collections bring together Taiwan’s wildlife, geology, and cultural traditions. One treasured object is a yellow tiger flag on a blue background.

Also nearby, Taipei Guest House (臺北賓館) began as the Japanese governor-general’s residence. Construction started in eighteen ninety-nine, and a redesign in nineteen eleven gave it today’s appearance. Sloping mansard roofs, classical columns, and elaborate Baroque decoration frame a building used for state banquets and visiting dignitaries. It first welcomed the general public on July fourth, two thousand six—a new chapter for this once secluded political setting.
