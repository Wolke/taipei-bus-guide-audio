Taiwan Television (臺視) is our next stop. Nearby, two very different venues tell stories about how Taipei makes room for culture, recreation, and big events.

Just eighty meters from the stop is Taipei City Stage (城市舞臺). Its story begins in nineteen sixty-four, with the founding of the Taipei Municipal Social Education Hall. Bringing arts and culture to the public was central to its mission.

There are two sides to this cultural space. The first-floor lobby and three exhibition rooms focus on displays, while the theater and arts plaza also host music, dance, and drama. So this is not just a place for watching a performance. Exhibitions are part of its everyday cultural role, too.

A renovation completed in two thousand three gave the performance venue a fresh start under the City Stage name. It became a more modern, professional theater designed for different kinds of performances. The organization also expanded its activities into other locations, making participation easier for more residents.

About three hundred eighty-seven meters from our stop is Taipei Arena (臺北小巨蛋). Before the arena, this site held the Taipei Municipal Baseball Stadium. The old stadium was demolished at the end of two thousand, making way for a multipurpose venue with fifteen thousand seats.

What is especially interesting here is the flexibility. Retractable seating allows the main floor to change with the event, and its central area can even become an ice surface for competition. Sports and performances share the same adaptable home.

The separate Ice Land rink serves both recreational skaters and athletes training for national competition. Its ice measures sixty-one meters by thirty meters, with room for four hundred skaters and seating for eight hundred spectators. Behind the fun is serious sporting equipment, including protective padding for short-track speed skating and boards for ice hockey. It is a place for both a casual skate and highly focused training.
