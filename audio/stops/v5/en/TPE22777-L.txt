Next stop is Xihu Library, Huguang Church (西湖圖書館（湖光教會）).

There is a small corner of Neihu nearby with two quite different faces. About one hundred and twenty-one meters from this stop is the Neihu Seven Three Seven Shopping District (內湖737商圈). The interesting thing here is not the length of the street. It is how the area changes from morning to evening.

In the morning, this is Lishan Market (麗山市場), where local residents come to buy groceries. Think of it as a glimpse of the neighborhood’s everyday shopping routine. The same area takes on a different role at night, when it becomes a destination for food.

The district is not especially long, but it brings together a variety of distinctive Taiwanese snacks and restaurants. So, rather than one single attraction, the appeal is the choice gathered into a relatively short stretch. Visitors come to explore the food, and office workers also stop by after work to browse and find something to eat. That mix of daily shopping and evening dining is what gives this place its two identities.

For a little geographical context, the district is also about a ten-minute walk from Exit One of MRT Gangqian Station (港墘站). Its convenient location helps draw people here.

And there is more nearby than food. Huguang Church has a European-style appearance, a detail to notice when looking around the neighborhood. Taipei Flower Market (臺北花卉市場) is also close to the district, as is the scenic lake, Bihu (碧湖).

Together, these nearby places give this part of Neihu a varied character: a local market in the morning, a compact food destination in the evening, and church architecture, flowers, and lake scenery in the surrounding area. It is a neighborhood whose attractions are not all of the same kind.
