MRT Dongmen Station (捷運東門站) is our upcoming stop. Nearby, about one hundred and sixty meters away, is a small surviving piece of Taipei’s history: the Taipei Prison Wall Remains (臺北監獄圍牆遺蹟).

These walls stand beside the telecommunications bureau on Jinshan South Road. The prison was first built around the nineteen tens, during Japanese rule. Today, only a few sections of its tall northern and southern walls remain, still carrying something of the prison’s somber atmosphere.

Here’s a detail worth noticing: much of the stone came from Taipei’s old city walls, which were dismantled during the nineteen tens. So these prison walls preserve materials from an even earlier chapter of the city’s past.

The prison itself had a radial layout, with sections spreading out from a central point. That design followed an international prison-building trend of the nineteenth century. Now a designated municipal historic site, the surviving walls are a reminder of Taiwan’s modern prison history.
