Next stop, Neihu Senior High School (內湖高中).

Nearby, just one hundred and forty-three meters from the stop, is Guo Ziyi Memorial Hall, also known as Neihu Red House (郭子儀紀念堂／內湖紅樓).

This hillside home belonged to the Guo family, whose member served as Neihu’s first village head during Japanese rule. Built around nineteen nineteen, it reflects the grand residential style of that period.

The house faces south, with woodland wrapping around three sides. Its red brick walls are decorated with washed stone and colorful ceramic tiles imported from Japan. Look at the front, and you’ll find windows in different shapes, a curved balcony, and flowing decorative curves inspired by Baroque design.

Inside, wooden beams support the floors. There are also special beams for hanging traditional lanterns and an incense burner dedicated to the Heavenly God.

With its roughly T-shaped layout and mix of brick and timber, this is a distinctive piece of Neihu’s architectural history.
