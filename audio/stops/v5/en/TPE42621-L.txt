MRT Jiantan Station, Zhongshan (捷運劍潭站), is our upcoming stop. This neighborhood brings together inventive theater spaces, a quiet historic shrine, and the flavors and history of Shilin.

Just one hundred meters from the stop is Taipei Performing Arts Center (臺北表演藝術中心). Its design invites a different way of thinking about theater. The Blue Box, Globe Playhouse, and Grand Theater can work separately or connect to create a larger performance space. There is also a Public Loop through the building. It reveals tucked-away theater spaces and routes used by performers and staff, then includes an open viewing platform overlooking the Shilin area. Art here is meant to be part of everyday life, not something kept behind the curtain.

Across from the MRT station, Yuanshan Water God Shrine (圓山水神社) offers a quieter story. Concrete steps lead toward a reservoir and this shrine from the Japanese colonial period. Look for original stone lanterns, the shrine’s name monument, a water basin, and two guardian lion-dogs. Its layout remains largely intact. The nearby Yuanshan Reservoir was an important point in the Caoshan water supply system.

Then comes Shilin Night Market (士林觀光夜市), Taipei’s largest night market. It spreads across streets around the traditional Yangming Theater and the reopened Shilin Market on Jihe Road. Food and shopping draw the crowds. Think enormous golden fried chicken cutlets, Shilin sausages, juicy pan-fried buns, and warming pork rib soup with medicinal herbs.

Within the market stands Shilin Cixian Temple (士林慈諴宮), dedicated to Mazu. Its predecessor began in seventeen ninety-six on donated land. Fighting and fire destroyed it in eighteen fifty-nine. When residents rebuilt their neighborhood elsewhere, they placed the temple at its center. Today, look for five courtyards, finely painted door figures, and qilin reliefs dating to eighteen twenty-eight. A stone wall image beside the main hall recalls old Shilin streets, connecting the neighborhood around us with the one that came before.
