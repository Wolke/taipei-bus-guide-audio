MRT Ximen Station (捷運西門站) is our next stop. Nearby, two landmarks tell very different stories about Taipei.

First, there’s Rainbow Six (6號彩虹), the rainbow pavement at MRT exit six. It was created in September twenty nineteen, the same year Taiwan became the first country in Asia to legalize same-sex marriage. It celebrates Taipei’s commitment to human rights, gender equality, and inclusion, and has become a popular place for photos.

Then there’s The Red House (西門紅樓), built in nineteen oh eight by Japanese architect Kondo Juro. This was Taiwan’s first government-built public market. Its distinctive design combines an eight-sided entrance building with a cross-shaped main building.

Over the years, it has served as a market, a storytelling venue, a cinema, and a theater. Today, it’s a center for arts and creative work, with exhibitions, Taiwanese design goods, a teahouse, and performance spaces. On weekends, creative markets add another chapter to this historic landmark’s story.
