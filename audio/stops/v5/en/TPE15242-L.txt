Dapu Street (大埔街) is our upcoming stop. Around here, Taipei’s stories unfold through birdsong, market buildings, night market food, and streets that still carry the shape of an earlier city.

Bird Street (和平西路三段鳥店街), on Section Three of Heping West Road, brings a traditional specialty trade to life. Shops sell birds, feed, and all kinds of birdkeeping equipment. The cages are a particular attraction. International visitors come looking for their distinctive Eastern style. Even without shopping, there is plenty to hear, with thousands of birds adding their voices to the street.

Nanjichang Night Market (南機場夜市) has a name that remembers a very different landscape. During Japanese rule, this area held a military airfield. Later came military dependents’ villages and public housing. As more people passed through, the market gradually grew. Today, Michelin-recommended snacks include chicken rice, shaobing flatbread, stinky tofu, and sesame oil chicken. There are also freshly wrapped, freshly pan-fried buns.

Taipei Botanical Garden (臺北植物園) offers another kind of richness: roughly two thousand plant species across about eight hectares. It was an important botanical research site during Japanese rule. Its themed sections include ferns, succulents, palms, and a lotus pond. A herbarium built in nineteen twenty-four was Taiwan’s first building specifically designed to store dried plant specimens for identification.

Xinfu Market (新富町文化市場), completed in nineteen thirty-five, shows how market design responded to new hygiene standards. Look for its unusual horseshoe layout. The central courtyard brought daylight and ventilation into the shopping space, while the layout helped people move through it smoothly.

Finally, Bopiliao Historic Block (剝皮寮歷史街區) preserves Qing-era street patterns and traditional shophouses. Red brick walls, arched covered walkways, and carved window details reward a closer look. Today, exhibitions, educational activities, and arts events connect those historic spaces with community life. Here, the buildings themselves help tell the story of how this part of Taipei developed.
