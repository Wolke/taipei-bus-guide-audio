National Palace Museum (故宮博物院) is our next stop. Nearby, there’s a peaceful green space with a different cultural story to share.

The Indigenous Peoples Cultural Park (原住民文化主題公園) is about one hundred and fifty-seven meters from the stop. This spacious park brings Taiwan’s diverse Indigenous cultures into an outdoor setting, with gentle paths, lakeside scenery, and shady corners.

Look for patterned paving that leads from the entrance toward the water. Sculptures include people pounding rice, designs inspired by hands and human figures, and statues of bears and sika deer. Together, they reflect cultural memories and the close relationship between Indigenous peoples and the forests and mountains.

Along the wooden lakeside walkway, bald cypress trees cast reflections on the water. Their foliage changes from green to gold or orange-red with the seasons. Water lilies and ginger lilies grow around the ecological pond. Wide, smooth paths also make this a welcoming place for wheelchairs, strollers, and relaxed family walks.
