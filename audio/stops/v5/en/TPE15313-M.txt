Zhongshan Market (中山市場) is our next stop. Around here, Taipei brings together Japanese food traditions, contemporary art, and cinema.

Just seventy-six meters from the stop is the Zhongshan North Road Tiaotong Shopping District (中山北路條通商圈). Its character reaches back to the Japanese colonial period. Today, the lanes still have izakayas, bars, and restaurants serving Japanese home cooking, from simmered beef and potatoes to eel rice. Qitiao Tong is also the birthplace of Taiwan’s yakiniku barbecue culture.

Nearby, Beyond Gallery (非畫廊) opened in January two thousand eight. It champions Taiwanese contemporary art while introducing international artists, creating connections between Taiwan and the wider art world.

Then there’s SPOT Taipei Film House (光點台北電影館), a white, two-story former residence of American ambassadors. Its columns and broad verandas frame a building now devoted to film culture. Inside, an intimate eighty-eight-seat cinema screens art and independent films. There’s also a bookstore focused on cinema and cities, and a café beside old garden trees.
