Shilin Market, Ming Chuan Hall (士林市場（銘傳會館）), is our next stop.

Just nearby is Shilin Tourist Night Market (士林觀光夜市), the largest night market in Taipei. Close to MRT Jiantan Station, it spreads across streets around the old Yangming Theater and the Shilin Market building on Jihe Road. Food and shopping draw the crowds, and the snacks offer a delicious introduction to Taiwan’s night market culture. Think oversized fried chicken cutlets, Shilin sausages, juicy pan-fried buns, and steaming bowls of herbal pork rib soup. There are spring rolls and scallion pancakes, too.

Within the night market is Shilin Cixian Temple (士林慈諴宮), dedicated to Mazu. Its predecessor was founded in seventeen ninety-six, but was destroyed by fire in eighteen fifty-nine. The temple’s present site was planned at the center of the rebuilt neighborhood. Its traditional southern Fujian layout includes five courtyards. Look for the carved dragon column and finely painted door guardians, details that reward a closer look.
