Museum, Guanqian (博物館（館前）), is our next stop. There’s plenty of Taipei’s history close by!

First, the National Taiwan Museum (國立臺灣博物館) is Taiwan’s oldest museum. Its main building is a national historic monument, completed during Japanese rule. Look at those grand columns and the Roman-style dome. Inside, sunlight filters through a stained-glass skylight above the lofty hall. The collections explore Taiwan’s wildlife, minerals, and cultures. One treasured object is a flag showing a yellow tiger against a blue background.

Nearby, Chongqing South Road Book Street (重慶南路書店街) has long drawn readers and students. At its busiest, it had more than a hundred bookstores and publishers. There are fewer today, but it still has Taipei’s highest concentration of bookstores.

Then there’s Taiwan Provincial City God Temple (臺灣省城隍廟), on Wuchang Street. Dedicated principally to the City God, it also houses Buddhist and other folk deities. Incense and quiet chanting create a peaceful contrast with the busy streets outside.
