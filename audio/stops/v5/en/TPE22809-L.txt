Xinyi and Jianguo Intersection (信義建國路口) is our next stop. Around here, flowers, forest paths, and jade collections offer three very different ways to enjoy Taipei.

First, there’s Jianguo Holiday Flower Market (建國假日花市), about two hundred meters from the stop. Its setting is part of the story. Beneath an elevated road, an ordinary parking area becomes a weekend world of greenery. More than two hundred stalls fill over eight thousand square meters. Look for the contrasts: delicate orchids, romantic roses, bold foliage, and tiny succulents.

The stallholders bring carefully selected plants, along with gardening supplies, seeds, and tea. They can also share practical growing advice. Sometimes portrait artists add another kind of creativity, while occasional family workshops let parents and children learn together.

About three hundred eighty meters away is Daan Forest Park (大安森林公園), known as the city’s lungs. It covers nearly twenty-six hectares, but its smaller landscapes deserve attention too. A natural pond lies in the northwest, with planted islands and koi swimming through the water. Farther south, bamboo and palms give different parts of the park their own character.

At the center, an outdoor music stage has naturally sloping audience space for almost a thousand people. Nearby plantings include fragrant gardenias and osmanthus. Since twenty fourteen, the park’s friends foundation has supported ecological work, balancing tree growth with biodiversity.

Then there’s Jianguo Holiday Jade Market (建國假日玉市), about five hundred forty meters from our stop. This market also sits beneath the elevated road. It began beside the old Guanghua Market and moved here in nineteen eighty-nine.

Its displays range from antique Chinese jade to crystal, pearls, agate, and distinctive Taiwan jade from the east coast. Religious objects and decorative pieces add to the variety. Think of it as an open jade museum: a place to notice craftsmanship, and the deep cultural affection surrounding this stone.
