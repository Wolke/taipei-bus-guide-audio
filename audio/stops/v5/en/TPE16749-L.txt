Shilin Market, Ming Chuan Hall (士林市場，銘傳會館) is our upcoming stop. Around here, night market food, temple history, modern theatre, and a quiet old shrine sit surprisingly close together.

Just nearby is Shilin Night Market (士林觀光夜市), Taipei’s largest night market. It has two main areas: the streets around the old Yangming Theater, including Danan Road, and Shilin Market at number one hundred and one, Jihe Road. Shopping brings in crowds, but food is a big attraction. Think oversized chicken cutlets, crisp outside and tender inside, fragrant Shilin sausages, and pan-fried buns filled with hot broth. Another specialty wraps a small pastry inside a larger flatbread, bringing sweet and salty flavours together.

Within the market is Shilin Cixian Temple (士林慈諴宮), dedicated to Mazu. Its story begins in seventeen ninety-six, when a landowner donated land for its predecessor. That temple burned during fighting between Zhangzhou and Quanzhou groups in eighteen fifty-nine. Afterwards, residents supported building a new street elsewhere, with the temple at its centre. Look for the entrance hall’s layered roof and curling ridges. Inside, two halls and their connecting spaces create five courtyards. The qilin reliefs date from eighteen twenty-eight, while a stone wall beside the main hall recalls old Shilin’s streets.

Nearby, Taipei Performing Arts Center (臺北表演藝術中心) offers a different kind of design story. Its three theatres can operate separately or connect into a larger performance space. The Public Loop reveals normally hidden corners and performers’ circulation routes, with an open viewing platform overlooking Shilin.

Finally, across from MRT Jiantan Station is Yuanshan Water Shrine (圓山水神社). This Japanese-era shrine retains its original stone lanterns, name monument, water basin, and paired guardian dogs. Steps lead towards the reservoir beside it, an important part of the Caoshan water system. Only a road separates this quiet setting from the Shilin shopping district. That contrast is part of what makes this neighbourhood so fascinating.
