NTU Hospital (臺大醫院) is our upcoming stop. This neighborhood has stories to tell about medicine in Taiwan, and some impressive architecture to match.

Just one hundred and fifty meters from the stop is National Taiwan University Hospital’s West Site (國立臺灣大學醫學院附設醫院西址). Designed by Kondo Juro, its two-story building combines red brick with reinforced concrete in a Renaissance style. The layout is symmetrical, with a central corridor linking clinics and wards. And this is not simply a historic landmark: the buildings still serve outpatients and house some hospital wards.

Nearby, the Museum of Medical Humanities (醫學人文博物館) occupies former medical school buildings. The main building was completed in nineteen thirteen, and much of its original appearance survives despite several restorations. Inside, elegant arches and carved details frame a different kind of medical story. Permanent exhibitions explore birth, the origins of Taiwan’s people, and their struggles with disease. The museum itself opened in two thousand eight.
