Xiaonanmen (小南門) is our next stop. There are two nearby places with quite different stories to tell.

First is Chongxi Gate (重熙門), also known as the Little South Gate. Taipei was one of the last walled cities built in Taiwan under Qing rule. Its walls are gone, but several gates remain. Unlike the usual four gates of a Chinese walled city, Taipei had five. This extra gate makes it an unusual example. The tower was later rebuilt in a northern Chinese palace style, while the base still keeps its original appearance.

Nearby, Nishi Honganji Square (西本願寺廣場) offers a quieter setting beside the busy city streets. Its historic buildings bring together Japanese and Western design. A bell tower stands on a small hill, and a traditional Japanese residence preserves its dark timber character. Another hall combines red brick, Western wooden roof framing, and a Japanese roof. Together, these reused historic spaces connect the lively culture of Ximending with Wanhua’s rich past.
