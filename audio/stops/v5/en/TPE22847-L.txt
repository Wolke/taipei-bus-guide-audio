Neihu Administration Building (內湖行政大樓) is our upcoming stop. Nearby, about one hundred and forty-one meters away, is the Taipei City Fire Department Disaster Prevention Science Education Center (臺北市政府消防局防災科學教育館). Its subject is serious, but its approach is hands-on: helping people understand emergencies by experiencing simulated situations.

The story behind the center begins with a disaster in Japan. On January seventeenth, nineteen ninety-five, the Great Hanshin Earthquake struck with a Richter magnitude of seven point two. It killed more than six thousand people and injured more than forty thousand. More than half a million buildings were completely or partly destroyed. Those devastating losses brought the importance of disaster education into sharp focus.

Later that same year, on July tenth, Taipei’s reorganized Fire Department was established. The department began planning Taiwan’s first disaster prevention science education center. The aim was to make disaster preparedness something residents could understand through experience, rather than simply through instructions.

That idea shapes what visitors encounter here. Computers, electronic systems, and mechanical equipment recreate disaster conditions for people to explore through practical activities. The center also introduces the history and characteristics of different disasters. It connects that background with knowledge about taking shelter and responding to fires, floods, earthquakes, and typhoons.

What is worth paying attention to is the link between each simulated situation and everyday life. The equipment is a teaching tool, not just a demonstration of technology. Learning through participation is intended to make emergency knowledge easier to carry into ordinary routines.

And the lesson is meant to travel beyond the center. Visitors are encouraged to share what they have learned with friends and family. That is the larger purpose of this nearby place: helping practical knowledge spread from one person to another, so more people can respond when disaster threatens.
