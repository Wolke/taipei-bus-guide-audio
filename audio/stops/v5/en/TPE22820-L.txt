Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall (中正紀念堂) is our upcoming stop. Around here, monumental architecture, performing arts, and stories of the sea sit within a few hundred meters of one another.

Let’s begin with the memorial hall, about two hundred and thirty-four meters from the stop. Chiang Kai-shek died in nineteen seventy-five, and construction began the following year. The site had previously housed army headquarters, once the largest military compound in central Taipei. There is even a story that gold brought from mainland China was stored here.

Look for the white walls and blue roof. Those colors echo the national flag, while the building rises seventy meters above the memorial park. Inside, the dome carries a white sun with twelve rays. The bronze statue faces west, toward the Presidential Office and mainland China. The square tells another chapter of Taiwan’s history: as democracy developed, it became a gathering place for democratic movements.

Nearby is the National Theater and Concert Hall (國家兩廳院), completed in nineteen eighty-seven as Taiwan’s first national-level cultural complex. Its two main buildings borrow from Chinese palace architecture. Notice the yellow roof tiles, sweeping eaves, red columns, and colorful arches. Behind that grand exterior are four performance halls, alongside a cultural gallery and a performing arts library. Programs range from classical works to experimental productions, with major international arts festivals held each year.

Then there’s the Evergreen Maritime Museum (長榮海事博物館), about two hundred and sixty meters from our stop. Its ship models trace a remarkable journey, from dugout canoes to sailing vessels and mechanically powered ships. Taiwan’s bamboo rafts appear alongside ceremonial king boats with religious significance. Marine paintings span the eighteenth through twentieth centuries, showing everything from wartime seas to sailboats on shimmering water. Navigation instruments and interactive multimedia displays add another perspective: how people find their way across the ocean.
