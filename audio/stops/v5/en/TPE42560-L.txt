MRT Shilin Station, Zhongzheng (捷運士林站中正), is our upcoming stop. Around here, temple architecture, outdoor sculpture, and traditional pastries each have a story to tell.

About two hundred and sixty-four meters from the stop is Shilin Shennong Temple (士林神農宮). Its grand appearance has a surprisingly practical explanation. Originally called Zhilan Temple, it honored the Earth God. During the Qianlong reign, residents rebuilt it with swallowtail roof decorations reserved for gods of imperial rank. Their devotion had made the building too grand for the rules.

The solution? They made Shennong, a deity of imperial standing, the principal god, helping protect the temple from demolition. Today, Shennong occupies the center of the upper floor of the main hall, while the Earth God remains below.

Look for the stone dragon columns and carved stone windows, among the older features preserved through repeated rebuilding. Shennong’s faces also carry meaning. A red face represents good health from eating cultivated grains. A black face recalls his sacrifice in tasting poisonous plants while searching for medicinal herbs.

Fulin Park (福林公園), about two hundred and fifteen meters from the stop, offers a different kind of visual storytelling. Completed in nineteen ninety-one, this triangular park features six sculpture groups by Li Zhih-wen. They turn images of the sun, clouds, mountains, waterfalls, water, and a well into abstract, three-dimensional forms inspired by pictographic writing. Water is cast in bronze; the other five use natural stone. Their appearance changes with your viewing distance.

Then there is the Kuo Yuan Ye Museum of Cake and Pastry (郭元益糕餅博物館), about three hundred and seventy-nine meters away. Its Shilin branch opened in two thousand two, on the fourth and fifth floors of the company headquarters at the original shop site. The exhibitions connect a century of pastry history with wedding, funeral, and celebration customs. A hands-on baking area adds another way to explore that heritage.
