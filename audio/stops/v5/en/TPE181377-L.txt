Taipei Children’s Amusement Park (兒童新樂園) is our next stop. This part of Shilin brings play and discovery close together, with two family attractions just a short distance from the stop.

The amusement park is about one hundred and nineteen metres away. It has thirteen major rides, but there is more here than the rides alone. Different areas cater to different ages, alongside free play spaces, family activities, themed performances, and a seasonal water play area. That makes it a place where younger and older children can find different ways to enjoy themselves.

The Ferris wheel is the park’s visual highlight. From up high, the view stretches across the grounds and the surrounding Shilin area. Around dusk, the lights add another layer to the scene. Down below, food outlets, a convenience store, and souvenir shops cover everyday needs. Nursing rooms, accessible routes, and resting seats also help families travelling with pushchairs or older relatives. EasyCard and iPASS can be used for admission, rides, and purchases at designated shops.

Nearby is the National Taiwan Science Education Center (國立臺灣科學教育館), about one hundred and fifty-seven metres from our stop. Here, the guiding idea is simple: learn by doing.

Permanent exhibitions occupy the third through sixth floors. Their subjects range from life science and mathematics to matter, energy, and Earth’s systems. Models, interactive equipment, and simulations turn abstract ideas into things visitors can explore.

One especially memorable example is the aerial bicycle, which uses a high wire setup to introduce balance. Earthquake simulation platforms demonstrate different shaking intensities and connect that experience with disaster preparedness. Younger children have a dedicated discovery space, too.

Changing special exhibitions and workshops extend the experience into model making, materials experiments, and science crafts. Together, these two neighbours offer a lovely contrast: the pleasure of play, and the pleasure of finding out how things work.
