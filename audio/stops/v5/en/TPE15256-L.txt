Beiping and Linsen Road Intersection (北平林森路口) is our next stop. This is bus three hundred and seven, and nearby are three places with stories of art, architecture, and a memorable visit to Taiwan.

About a hundred and fifty metres away is the Pu Tian-Sheng Sculpture Memorial Museum (蒲添生雕塑紀念館). Behind the busy surroundings of Linsen North Road stands a Japanese-style wooden house more than eighty years old. This was the sculptor’s home and workplace in his later years. Its special treasure is the studio, which he designed and supervised himself, using high-quality cypress wood. Think of this less as a grand museum and more as an introduction to an artist through his own living space. There is an outdoor sculpture garden, along with rooms displaying works and personal historical objects. His sculpture brings together classical realism and a deep interest in human life.

Around two hundred and fifty metres from our stop is Zhongshan Church (中山教會). Look closely at its entrance: the pillars record that its foundation was laid in March, nineteen thirty-seven. Built by Japanese residents, it originally served an Anglican congregation living in the surrounding district. Its design draws on English Gothic churches. Pointed arches and buttresses are details to notice, while the roof forms a cross when seen from above.

A little farther away, Yixian Park (逸仙公園) remembers Sun Yat-sen’s visit in nineteen thirteen. He stayed at the Japanese restaurant that is central to the park’s history, and met Taiwan’s governor-general there to discuss political affairs. Built in nineteen hundred, the restaurant took its name from plum trees planted in its garden. It once welcomed senior officials, business leaders, and prominent social figures. The rectangular Japanese-style building has a roof of dark tiles. In this small park near Taipei Main Station, a former dining venue preserves a connection to an important visitor and the city’s earlier social world.
