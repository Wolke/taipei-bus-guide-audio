Our next stop is Huashan Park, Zhongshan Church (華山公園、中山教會).

Just over a hundred meters from the stop is Zhongshan Church, a church built by the Japanese. It originally served a Japanese Anglican congregation, giving Japanese Christians living around what was then Taisho Street a place to worship.

There is a little piece of its history right at the entrance. An inscription on the arched gateway records the laying of the foundation in March, nineteen thirty-seven.

The outside takes its inspiration from English Gothic churches. Notice the pointed arches and the buttresses, those supports along the walls. The roof has another distinctive feature: viewed from above, it forms the shape of a cross.

The church’s architectural details have remained well preserved, including its corner stones and roof structure. Together, these features give the building its architectural value. It is a nearby place where the story is told through both an inscription and the building itself.
