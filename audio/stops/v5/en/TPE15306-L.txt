Nanjing–Dunhua Intersection, Taipei Arena (南京敦化路口（小巨蛋）), is our upcoming stop. This part of Taipei brings together two very different kinds of performance: major arena events and the more intimate world of theatre, music, and dance.

Only about fifty metres from the stop is Taipei Arena (臺北小巨蛋), at the meeting of Dunhua North Road and Nanjing East Road in Songshan District. Before the arena, this was the home of Taipei Municipal Baseball Stadium. The old stadium was demolished at the end of the year two thousand, making way for a multipurpose venue with fifteen thousand seats.

The interesting thing here is how much the space can change. Retractable seating lets the main arena adapt to different events, and its central floor can even become a competition ice surface. That flexibility supports both international sporting competitions and performances by arts groups from Taiwan and overseas.

There is also a dedicated skating rink within the complex. Its ice measures sixty-one metres by thirty metres, with room for four hundred skaters and seating for eight hundred spectators. It serves recreational visitors, but also skilled skaters and athletes training for national teams. So this is not just somewhere to watch sport; it is also a place where people practise it.

About four hundred and seventy metres from our stop is Taipei City Stage (城市舞臺). Its story reaches back to nineteen sixty-four, when the Taipei Municipal Social Education Hall was established to promote arts and culture.

After renovations finished in two thousand three, the performance venue took the City Stage name and began a new chapter as a more modern, professional theatre. Music, dance, and drama are part of its programme, while the hall’s lobby and three exhibition rooms provide spaces for exhibitions.

Two nearby venues, then, with different histories: one grew from a baseball ground, and the other from a centre for public arts education.
