Shuangxi Park (雙溪公園) is our upcoming stop. About two hundred and twenty-three metres from the stop, this garden offers a quiet contrast to the busy city around it.

Its name comes from two streams that flow from north to south and meet in this part of Taipei. The park opened in nineteen seventy-four, and its public grounds cover about two hectares.

The layout is flat and triangular, but the scenery brings together the feel of mountains and water in a traditional Chinese garden. Covered walkways link a pavilion in the middle of the pond with waterside structures, a stone grotto, a zigzag bridge, and an artificial mountain with a waterfall.

That winding bridge is just one part of a connected garden design, with different features gathered around the water. The park combines a modern sense of beauty with an old-fashioned charm, creating a peaceful pocket among the surrounding city streets.
