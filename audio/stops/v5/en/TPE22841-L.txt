Our next stop is Xindong Street Entrance (新東街口). There are two nearby places to discover here, each with a different way of bringing water into city life.

First, about two hundred and fifty-five meters from the stop, is Fumin Ecological Park (富民生態公園). This is one of Taipei’s few parks focused on aquatic ecology. It took around five years to develop and was completed at the end of two thousand two.

The park covers about eight thousand four hundred and thirteen square meters, but its central feature is a waterway nearly one hundred meters long. Think of it as a northern Taiwan stream in miniature. Following its course gives you a closer look at several different watery habitats, rather than just one ornamental pond.

Downstream from the river habitat is a pond area, with floating water ferns, water lilies, cattails, and Taiwan yellow water lilies. At the far end, a floodplain habitat supports still more aquatic plants.

And this is a place for more than looking. Birds, amphibians, and insects are part of the scene. Frog calls, birdsong, and flowing water form its soundtrack, while ginger lily and osmanthus bring floral scents. That combination offers a gentler contrast to Taipei’s busy traffic.

About six hundred meters from the stop is Rainbow Riverside Park (彩虹河濱公園), beside the Keelung River (基隆河). Here, the striking feature is Rainbow Bridge (彩虹橋), with its red, sweeping S-shaped structure, a favorite subject for photographers.

The riverside plaza beneath the bridge takes a romantic turn. Its lovers’ walkway presents “I love you” in twelve languages. There are also seats for couples, a three-dimensional LOVE sculpture, and love locks.

So these nearby spaces offer two very different waterside scenes: a miniature stream rich in living things, and a riverfront where bridge design, photography, and romance come together.
