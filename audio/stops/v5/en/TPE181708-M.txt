Taipei Main Station, Chongqing (臺北車站（重慶）), is our next stop. Nearby, two historic buildings tell us a little about how Taipei grew.

First is Taipei Beimen Post Office (臺北北門郵局), about one hundred and eighty meters from the stop. As the railway network developed, postal services moved here to be close to the station. The original wooden building was lost to fire, and a reinforced concrete replacement followed in nineteen twenty-nine. It is a substantial four-story building with clean lines, an arched entrance, and a hall rising through two floors. Inside, delicate classical carvings decorate the columns and ceiling.

A little farther away is North Gate, also called Cheng'en Gate (承恩門). Of old Taipei’s five city gates, this is the only one that preserves its original Qing-era appearance in the southern Fujian style. It once formed an important passage between the walled city and Dadaocheng. The gate is currently temporarily closed, with reopening subject to an official announcement.
