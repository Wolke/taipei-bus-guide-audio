The National Theater and Concert Hall (國家兩廳院) opened in 1987 as Taiwan’s first national cultural complex. Its two main buildings echo Chinese palace architecture, with yellow roof tiles, sweeping eaves, and red columns. Inside, four performance halls host music, theater, and dance. A performing arts library and guided tours offer more ways to explore.

Nearby, the National Chiang Kai-shek Memorial Hall (國立中正紀念堂) commemorates the Republic of China’s first president. Architect Yang Cho-cheng, who also designed the Grand Hotel, created this blue-and-white landmark. It rises 70 meters above its park. As Taiwan’s democracy developed, the square became a gathering place for democratic movements.

The Evergreen Maritime Museum (長榮海事博物館) tells stories of the sea through ship models, paintings, and interactive displays. Its collection ranges from dugout canoes to powered vessels, including Taiwan’s bamboo rafts and ceremonial king boats. Navigational instruments and multimedia exhibits help visitors explore how people find their way at sea.
