Songshan Fengtian Temple (松山奉天宮) began with a simple bamboo building and the efforts of local residents. In 1953, community leaders and elders raised funds to establish a temple dedicated to the Jade Emperor. That modest beginning grew into a place where religious life, mountain walks, and everyday neighborhood activity come together.

The temple stands on Hushan (虎山), or Tiger Mountain, part of the Four Beasts Mountains (四獸山). It is close to City Hall and the Xinyi shopping district (信義商圈), yet its hillside setting offers a quieter atmosphere. From this elevated area, the city spreads out below, with mountain peaks visible in the distance.

The Tiger Mountain trail passes through the temple area, bringing together hikers and nearby residents. Their activity has helped create a lively market serving daily needs, including food and clothing. This is a neighborhood where a mountain outing and ordinary daily errands share the same setting. The temple area is also a place for leisure and a moment of reflection away from the bustle.

Another important religious site is Songshan Cihui Temple (松山慈惠堂), at the foot of Fushoushan (福壽山). Founded by the devoted Taoist laywoman Guo Yezi, it is dedicated to the Queen Mother of the West. Its founding ideals emphasize compassion, spiritual cultivation, and helping others.

Here, the architecture gives the temple much of its character. The complex has five stories, with tower-style bell and drum buildings adding to its imposing appearance. Its halls follow traditional Chinese temple design while drawing on features of Ming and Qing palace architecture.

Red brick and yellow tiles stand among green pines and cypresses. From the second-floor main hall balcony, there are broad views across Taipei (臺北). Together, these two temples reveal a hillside neighborhood shaped by faith, community life, and the meeting of city and mountain.
